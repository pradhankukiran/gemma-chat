Differential Diagnosis & Workup

High priority differentials given the presentation of acute chest pain with radiation, diaphoresis, and cardiovascular risk factors:

1. Acute Coronary Syndrome (STEMI/NSTEMI) — Most likely given classic presentation. The combination of substernal pain with left arm radiation, diaphoresis, HTN, DM2, and tachycardia at HR 102 bpm strongly suggests ACS.

2. Aortic Dissection — Must rule out given HTN history and acute onset. Check for BP differential between arms. Current BP 158/94 mmHg.

3. Pulmonary Embolism — Consider given tachycardia and acute dyspnea if present.

Immediate workup:

STAT 12-lead ECG, Troponin I/T (serial q3h), CBC, BMP, PT/INR, Chest X-ray, and Type & Screen. Administer Aspirin 325mg chewable immediately if no contraindications. Establish IV access, continuous telemetry, and supplemental O2 if SpO2 <94%.
